The Challenge

Aquahacking Binational: Great Lakes & St. Lawrence 2026 challenged engineering teams to develop conceptual systems to monitor water quality issues in North American aquatic ecosystems. Our team focused on nutrients and nutrient cycling, addressing the problem of nitrogen and phosphorus inputs. High levels of agricultural and municipal nutrient run-off lead to eutrophication and toxic algal blooms, threatening local drinking water and ecological stability.

During the design phase, our team identified that nitrogen detection was comparatively easier using commercially available sensing approaches, while phosphorus presented a more difficult detection problem. To address this, we explored orthophosphate detection as a conceptual approach to phosphorus monitoring.

The core design challenge was to propose a buoy concept that could float in open water, harvest solar energy, and house electronic sensors capable of monitoring and transmitting nutrient levels to local conservation teams.

My Contributions

As part of the design group, my individual contributions focused on taking the team's conceptual ideas and translating them into technical drawings, analysis, and accessible formats:

CAD & Product Modeling

Helped develop the 3D CAD representation in SolidWorks by translating initial team sketch ideas into a physical model assembly, refining fitment and component layouts.

Stakeholder Salience Analysis

Investigated and categorized stakeholder groups using the Power, Legitimacy, and Urgency framework to understand design constraints for municipal and community organizations.

Risk Assessment

Collaborated with the team to identify conceptual operational risks and outline potential mitigation paths during the design stage.

Solution Development

Contributed to the solution description for nutrient detection, focusing on the mechanical layout and physical shielding of sensor housings.

Design Process Nomination: The engineering rigour applied to our risk assessment matrices, stakeholder analysis, and iterative physical modeling led to the project being nominated in the Design Process Category at the Aquahacking Binational competition.

Engineering Process & Analysis

Stakeholder Salience Model

During the planning phase, I analyzed the project stakeholders. Stakeholders are categorized based on three core attributes: Power (the ability to influence the project), Legitimacy (the authority to interact with the project), and Urgency (the immediate attention required by their concerns). This classification helped define our operational guidelines.

Stakeholder Group Power Legitimacy Urgency Classification
Conservation Authorities High High High Definitive (Must satisfy all operational standards)
Agricultural Landowners High High Medium Dominant (Requires active consultation on runoff)
Local Lake Communities Low High High Dependent (Needs accessible reporting dashboard)

Risk Assessment Matrix

Together with the design team, I developed a risk assessment matrix to identify environmental and mechanical vulnerabilities of an open-water sensor buoy and establish design mitigations.

Identified Risk Likelihood Impact Design Mitigation Strategy
Biofouling of Sensor Optics High Medium Protective sensor casing and manual cleaning intervals.
Watertight Seal Degradation Medium High Watertight compartments and rubber gaskets.
Mooring/Anchor Line Failure Low High Secured mooring attachments and counterweighted anchor design.
Power Shortage (Winter Days) Medium Medium Sizing solar cells based on regional solar exposure statistics.

Making the CAD Accessible

The final design was created in SolidWorks. To make the design easier for stakeholders without CAD software to review, I created a browser-based 3D viewer hosted through GitHub Pages.

The viewer allowed users to interact with the 3D model through a web browser, enabling stakeholders to pan, zoom, and rotate the 3D model geometry directly.
